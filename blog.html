Simple Online Safety Guides

No complicated computer talk. Just simple, practical rules that protect your family and hard-earned savings.

Everyday Safety

1. 5 Signs of a Scam Message (WhatsApp & SMS)

Every day, millions of people receive WhatsApp and text messages from strangers. Some look like lottery prizes, some claim to be electricity bill alerts, and others offer easy jobs. Here are the 5 obvious signs that a message is 100% fake:

  1. 1. It creates panic or gives a short deadline:
    Scammers love to write: "Electricity will be cut tonight at 9:30 PM" or "Your SIM card will be blocked in 2 hours". They create panic so you don't take time to ask your family for advice.
  2. 2. It comes from a personal 10-digit mobile number:
    Real companies like your electricity board, State Bank of India, or Amazon do NOT send official alerts from numbers like 9876543210. Official senders always have branded IDs like VK-SBI or AD-POWER.
  3. 3. It promises huge money for zero effort:
    Messages claiming you can earn ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 every day just by clicking "Like" on YouTube videos or rating hotels on Google are scams. Nobody pays thousands of rupees for clicking a like button!
  4. 4. It asks for your OTP or password:
    No real company or bank manager will EVER ask you to read your OTP over the phone or type it in a message.
  5. 5. You won a prize in a contest you never joined:
    Messages claiming you won ₹25 Lakhs in Kaun Banega Crorepati (KBC) or an iPhone lucky draw are always fake. If you didn't buy a lottery ticket, you cannot win a lottery!

What to do: Whenever you see any of these 5 signs, simply delete the message and block the sender. Never forward it to your family groups.

Bank Safety

2. How to Protect Your Bank Account Online (Simple Guide)

You don't need to be an engineer to keep your bank account safe. Just remember these 4 golden rules of online money safety:

  • Rule 1: UPI PIN is ONLY for paying money, NEVER for receiving money!
    This is the most common scam in India. A buyer on OLX or WhatsApp says: "I sent you ₹5,000. Enter your UPI PIN to accept it." Remember: Receiving money into your Google Pay, PhonePe, or Paytm NEVER requires typing your UPI PIN!
  • Rule 2: Never install "Screen Sharing" apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport):
    If someone claiming to be "Customer Care" tells you to download AnyDesk or QuickSupport on your phone to solve a problem, hang up! These apps allow scammers to see your screen and read your passwords and OTPs.
  • Rule 3: Keep your daily UPI limit low:
    In your bank app or UPI app, set your daily transaction limit to ₹10,000 or ₹20,000. That way, even in the worst case, nobody can drain your entire life savings.
  • Rule 4: Memorize the Cyber Police Helpline: 1930
    If money is ever debited from your bank account without your permission, dial 1930 within 2 hours. The police can freeze the scammer's bank account before they take out the cash!
Emergency Checklist

3. What to Do If You Clicked a Suspicious Link by Mistake

It happens to the best of us: you were distracted, tapped a link on WhatsApp, and suddenly realized it looks fishy. Don't panic! Follow these 4 immediate steps:

  1. Step 1: Close the browser tab immediately.
    Do not touch anything on the page, and do not press "Allow Notifications" or "Download".
  2. Step 2: Did you enter any password or PIN?
    If you typed your password, go to the official website from another app or computer and change your password immediately. If you entered your debit card or netbanking details, call your bank helpline to block your card immediately.
  3. Step 3: Check your Downloads folder.
    Open your phone's File Manager or Downloads folder. If you see any file downloaded recently (like an .apk file or file ending in .exe), delete it immediately! Do NOT tap on it to install.
  4. Step 4: Check if money was deducted:
    Check your bank SMS or open your banking app. If any unauthorized transaction occurred, immediately dial 1930 (National Cyber Helpline) and inform your bank.
